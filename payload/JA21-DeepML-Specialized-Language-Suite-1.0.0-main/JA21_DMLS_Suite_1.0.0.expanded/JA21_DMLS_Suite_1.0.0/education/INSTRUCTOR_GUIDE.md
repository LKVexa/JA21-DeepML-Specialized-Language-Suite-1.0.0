
# DeepML Specialized Instructor Guide

## Teaching principle

Teach every feature as a chain of domain claims and evidence. Syntax is introduced together with units, schemas, provenance, adapters, policy, diagnostics, lowering, authority, and replay behavior.

## Recommended rhythm

1. Read one small positive program.
2. Inspect its corpus record, units, hashes, expected stage, and relation evidence.
3. Compare an ordinary diagnostic program on the same topic.
4. Identify the earliest responsible rejection stage and bounded repair.
5. Inspect R12/MCRT evidence and replay requirements.
6. Complete a laboratory and publish a concise evidence note.

## Grading rubric

| Dimension | Excellent | Adequate | Needs revision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Domain semantics | Preserves units, schemas, adapters, assumptions, calibration, and authority | Mostly correct with minor omissions | Relies on labels or output appearance |
| Evidence | Uses IDs, hashes, stages, operator identity, provenance, R12/MCRT, and replay context | Uses partial evidence | Relies on unverified narrative claims |
| Diagnostics | Identifies the root stage and bounded repair | Correct stage but limited explanation | Generic or downstream-only error |
| Claim boundaries | Separates modeled, numerical, visual, judgement, and native evidence | Minor ambiguity | Presents modeled outcomes as executed proof |
| Publication | Navigable, versioned, path-safe, and integrity-checked | Usable but incomplete | Missing structure, provenance, or limitations |

## Certification handling

The corpus contains 1,000 holdouts. Keep them separate during ordinary instruction. Freeze the implementation before holdout evaluation and disclose any prior exposure.
